# FourHour

Automatic power-outage cover for small food sellers, paid through PayPal.

## What it does

USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service advises that perishable food in a refrigerator without power is safe for only about 4 hours ([Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/emergencies/keep-your-food-safe-during-emergencies)). A food truck owner, home baker or corner grocer with no generator loses their stock in a short outage, and ordinary business cover is built for long outages and pays slowly. With FourHour, a member saves their PayPal wallet once and pays a small monthly premium from it. A laptop or phone left on a charger at their premises becomes a **witness**: it keeps a signed, hash-chained log of when its charger loses power. When the county's outage index (DOE EAGLE-I, the share of customers without power) stays above a threshold for 4 hours and the member's own witness confirms the outage at their premises, the rule engine creates a claim and sends a PayPal Payout. No claim form. A witness on its own can never create a payout: a phone unplugged by hand while the grid is fine pays nothing.

## PayPal API calls

All calls go to the sandbox (`https://api-m.sandbox.paypal.com`); the service refuses to start with any other base URL. Code: `backend/internal/paypal/`.

| # | Endpoint | Purpose | Idempotency | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | `POST /v1/oauth2/token` | Access token, cached until shortly before expiry | none | `client.go` `accessToken` |
| P2 | `POST /v3/vault/setup-tokens` | Start saving the member's PayPal wallet (`usage_type: MERCHANT`) | `PayPal-Request-Id: vst-{policy_id}` | `client.go` `CreateSetupToken` |
| P3 | `POST /v3/vault/payment-tokens` | Exchange the approved setup token for a payment token | `PayPal-Request-Id: vpt-{policy_id}` | `client.go` `CreatePaymentToken` |
| P4 | `POST /v2/checkout/orders` | Charge a monthly premium against the vaulted wallet, buyer not present | `PayPal-Request-Id: prem-{policy_id}-{period}` | `client.go` `CreateOrder` |
| P5 | `POST /v1/payments/payouts` | Pay a claim to the member's PayPal account | `sender_batch_id: clm-{policy_id}-{outage_event_id}-{tier}` | `client.go` `CreatePayout` |
| P6 | `GET /v1/payments/payouts/{batch_id}` | Poll a payout until its item reaches a final status | read-only | `client.go` `GetPayoutBatch` |
| P8 | `POST /v1/notifications/verify-webhook-signature` | Verify a webhook before it changes anything | read-only | `p7p8.go` `VerifyWebhookSignature` |

Webhook endpoint: `POST /api/paypal/webhook` (`backend/internal/service/webhook.go`). Payout confirmation arrives by webhook or, when no public URL is registered, by polling P6.

Money rules: amounts are integer cents end to end; every money row is written locally with its idempotency key before the PayPal call; the database allows one payout per (policy, outage event, tier) and appends each money movement to the ledger once.

## The AI

FourHour's "AI" is a set of statistical and actuarial models, not a language model. No language model sets a price, an amount or a claim decision. Every number below is read from a file in `artifacts/`, generated by code in `ml/` from real data, with a `provenance` block naming the commit, data files and seed. Models are fitted on 2014–2022 and evaluated on 2023–2024, which they never saw; the split is enforced in one place (`ml/fourhour_ml/split.py`) and tested.

| Model | Fitted on | Out-of-sample result (2023–2024) | Artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Event reconstruction.** 15-minute EAGLE-I share-out per county; runs above a threshold; missing gaps up to 2 h bridged; partial-feed dropouts (a 90% fall that recovers within 2 h) treated as missing; runs within 72 h merged into one event | TX, FL, IL counties 2014–2024; 54 of 4,653 county-years below the 0.70 coverage floor are excluded from fitting | 97.2% of gaps inside large training events fall within the 2 h bridge (104 events examined). Timestamps confirmed as UTC against the Houston derecho | `data_quality.json` |
| **Trigger design.** Grid search over (`theta_lo`, `theta_hi`), trading wrong payouts against missed payouts | 2014–2022 | `theta_lo` = 0.20, `theta_hi` = 0.90. 0.90 is the **edge of the 190-pair grid, not an optimum**: at county scale most index-only payments would go to premises that had power, so the objective pushes index-only payment towards never paying. Cover in practice requires the witness | `trigger_params.json` |
| **Frequency.** Negative-binomial GLM of qualifying events per county-month, with month-of-year and state effects, against a Poisson GLM and the county's historical mean | 144 county-years | **Did not beat its baselines.** Deviance 130.23 (NB) vs 130.22 (Poisson); log score 0.158 for both. The NB dispersion is estimated near zero, so it collapses to the Poisson model. The historical-mean baseline scores infinite (it predicts zero for Harris, which then had the 2024 derecho and Beryl) | `backtest.json` |
| **Severity.** Generalised Pareto on run length beyond 4 h | 34 training events | KS statistic 0.146, p = 0.42 (optimistic: parameters estimated from the same data). P(24 h \| event): fitted 0.81, training 0.82, **test 0.68** | `backtest.json` |
| **Credibility.** Bühlmann–Straub blend of county and state rates | 22 counties | Between-county variance estimated as not positive, so **every credibility weight is 0**: county histories carry no signal beyond their state's rate at this event count | `backtest.json` |
| **Pricing.** Premium = expected annual payout / 12 × (1 + risk margin) + $1.00 expense load | 2014–2022 | Harris and Miami-Dade: 0.25 qualifying events a year; risk margin 47.9%. Monthly premium for $50 / $150 / $300 cover: $1.57 / $2.70 / $4.39. Cook County had no qualifying event in training, so its premium is the $1.00 expense load alone | `pricing_table.json` |
| **Solvency.** 1,000 simulated members over the three counties; whole historical years resampled per county to keep storm clustering; 20,000 simulations | 2014–2022 | Probability of ruin 12.3%; capital for a 1-in-200 year $16,864.92; aggregate stop-loss layer priced at $85.17 | `solvency.json` |
| **Backtest.** The frozen pool run through the test years | 2014–2022 models | 2023: no qualifying events, loss ratio 0.00. 2024: 2 events in Harris (the derecho and Hurricane Beryl), 408 simulated claims paid, **loss ratio 1.29**. Two years is far too few to judge a loss ratio | `backtest.json` |
| **Basis risk (India, ESMI).** Does a district index pay the right premises? Minute-level supply at individual premises; 8 districts with at least 8 active monitors; thresholds chosen on 2014-10-22 to 2016-11-24, scored on 2016-11-25 to 2018-12-31 | ESMI first half | Of 5,010 premises outages of 4 h or more: **index only** misses 70.4% and 35.3% of its payments are wrong. **Full rules with a perfect witness** miss 5.9% with 0% wrong. With 10% of witness logs missing: 10.9% missed, 1.7% wrong; with 30% missing: 23.5% missed, 6.0% wrong. The witness here is the premises' own monitor, so these are upper bounds | `basis_risk.json` |

Rules (from `SPEC.md` section 4): R1 index at `theta_hi` 4 h + witness confirms → pay 60% of cover; R2 index at `theta_hi` 4 h, no witness → pay 30%; R3 index at `theta_lo` 4 h + witness confirms → pay 60%; R4 index at `theta_hi` but the witness shows power stayed on → declined with reason; R5 witness reports an outage with the index quiet → no payout, flagged for review.

## What is real and what is simulated

| Real | Simulated |
|---|---|
| EAGLE-I outage records for Texas, Florida and Illinois, 2014–2024, and the replayed storm windows (labelled "real data") | Members, their premises and loss amounts (labelled "simulated") |
| ESMI premises-level supply records used in the basis-risk study | The replay clock: a historical storm replays at speed |
| Every model result, with its provenance | The pool in the backtest and solvency simulation |
| PayPal sandbox calls: vault, premium charge, payout, webhook verification. Money moves between sandbox accounts | The witness entries in a replay are real signed entries from a real browser, but replay mode maps them onto the simulated clock |

## Limitations

- **Cover in practice requires a witness.** Index-only payment (R2) needs at least 90% of a county's customers out, and 0.90 is the edge of the search grid, not a fitted optimum, so R2 almost never pays.
- **Outages affecting less than 20% of a county (`theta_lo`) are not covered**, even if the member's premises is dark. That is a limit of county-level data.
- **Basis risk.** A county index does not prove a particular fridge was off. The ESMI study measures this on Indian premises data, with a perfect witness, so its numbers are an upper bound on what a real witness achieves.
- **The witness can be spoofed on its own** (unplug the charger). That is why a witness can confirm or contradict an index event but never create one (R5).
- **Self-reported entries are weaker than sensed ones.** The witness page always offers "Power is out" / "Power is back" buttons, and they are the only option in browsers without the Battery Status API (Safari, iOS). Entries made this way are labelled "self-reported", but the rules treat them the same as sensed charger events.
- **R2/R4 incentive.** A member with no witness can still get 50% (R2), while a member whose witness shows power stayed on gets nothing (R4). That could discourage registering a witness. A production design would price the two cases differently.
- **The 24-hour tier is priced but not paid.** The premium includes the expected 24-hour top-up (`t24` in `pricing_table.json`), but this prototype pays the 4-hour tier only, so the quoted premium is higher than the cover it pays.
- **Small premiums carry a high share of fixed payment fees.** A premium of a few dollars a month leaves little after per-transaction fees; the $1.00 expense load is a team assumption, not a measured cost.
- **Thin evidence.** The frequency model did not beat its baselines, credibility weights are all 0, and the backtest has two test years and two qualifying events in the demo counties. EAGLE-I gaps and feed dropouts affect event lengths.
- The witness wrong-confirmation rate (0.10) and witness take-up (0.5) used in trigger design are team assumptions, not measured values; `trigger_params.json` reports how the chosen thresholds change across them.

## Framing

FourHour is the pricing, trigger and claims engine a licensed carrier would run; a sandbox prototype, not an insurance offer.

## Running it

Needs Go 1.27+, Node 20.19+ or 22.12+, and a PayPal sandbox app with Vault and Payouts enabled. Python 3.12+ is needed only to regenerate artifacts (the committed `artifacts/` are enough to run the app).

1. **Sandbox accounts** (developer.paypal.com → Sandbox): one business account with a positive balance (it funds payouts) and two personal accounts, one to pay premiums and one to receive payouts. On the app, enable Vault and Payouts.
2. **Configure.** Copy `.env.example` to `.env` at the repo root and fill it in. For a local run: `PAYPAL_BASE_URL=https://api-m.sandbox.paypal.com`, `APP_BASE_URL=http://localhost:8080`, `FRONTEND_URL=http://localhost:5173`, `VITE_API_BASE_URL=http://localhost:8080/api`, `VITE_USE_MOCK=false`. `PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID` can stay empty locally; payouts are then confirmed by polling.
3. **Backend:**
   ```
   cd backend
   go run ./cmd/fourhour -env-file ../.env
   ```
   Check `http://localhost:8080/api/health` lists six loaded artifacts.
4. **Frontend:**
   ```
   cd frontend
   npm ci
   npm run dev
   ```
   Open `http://localhost:5173`.
5. **Demo:** `/enrol` → pick Harris County and a cover → save PayPal with a sandbox personal account. On the policy page, open the witness link on a laptop on its charger (Chrome) and register it. On `/live`, replay `derecho-harris-2024`; unplug the charger when the index opens and leave it unplugged; after 4 simulated hours the claim is decided and the payout is sent, then confirmed. `spoof-quiet` shows R5: the charger is unplugged while the index stays quiet, and no payout is made.
6. **Tests:** `cd backend && go test ./...`; `cd ml && python -m pytest`; `cd frontend && npm test`.

Deploying to Render: see `DEPLOY.md`.
